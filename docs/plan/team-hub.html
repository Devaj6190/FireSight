Track requirement: "What we don't know" panel

An in-app screen and a demo beat. Each row gives the layer, its as-of date and the gap.

  • Building heights: from 2010 laser scans; outlines as of 2023-09.
  • Hydrants and cisterns: a 2018 SF DEM snapshot; positions may have changed.
  • Fire incidents: about 4 weeks behind. Calls: about 8 hours behind. Never shown as "live".
  • Units per floor: unit numbers mentioned in permits only, a lower bound.
  • No floor plans: stairs and standpipe locations are UNVERIFIABLE.
  • Sources disagree: the tax roll says 78 units, permits say 79, marked CONFLICT.
  • Inspection to statute: linking SFFD inspection types to the California law is our own inference.
  • NFPA 1660: mapped from the earlier 1620 text; 1660 itself is paywalled.
  • City map layers: state no license, so each pin carries its source and date.

Responsible data practices

  • Public records only, with no personal data. Fields with names, phones or emails are dropped at load.
  • Every pin carries its source, dataset ID, record ID and as-of date.
  • The AI sees only the fused building JSON. The validator drops uncited lines, and unknowns are said aloud.
  • The human approves or strikes each line before it's sent, and struck lines become regression tests.
  • The MCP server is read-only and returns typed, cited answers.
  • Attribution: DataSF (public domain), "© OpenStreetMap contributors" (ODbL), and SF DEM layers as of their dates.

Wording

  • Say "meets" only for California's inspection law (H&SC 13146.2) and the solar-pathway rule logic. Say "maps to NFPA 1660".
  • Say "published quotes", never "we interviewed".

Read this first

The idea

FireSight turns any San Francisco address into a cited, floor-by-floor 3D pre-plan in seconds, so the officer on a responding engine knows what's inside before the wheels stop.

For whoThe company officer (captain) on the first-due engine, reading the rig's tablet or a phone during the drive. First payer: pre-plan software vendors (First Due, Tablet Command, ESO). They buy our resolved building data over the API and MCP, so there's no slow public procurement.
ProblemWhat changes tactics is scattered across a dozen city datasets nobody can read en route: construction type, soft-story status, rooftop solar, unit counts, open fire violations, past fires. Pre-plans are drawn by hand and go stale. Modern rooms reach flashover in under 5 minutes, against about 29 for older rooms (UL), so there's no time to look things up.
Why nowNew hazards like rooftop solar, battery storage and EV chargers show up only in permit text. SF publishes laser-measured heights for all 177,023 buildings. NFPA folded its pre-incident planning standard into NFPA 1660 in 2024 (reported; we read the earlier 1620 text). California requires annual fire inspections of every apartment building and hotel (H&SC 13146.2). And coding agents can resolve city records into one building in seconds, which wasn't practical before.
Why usWe've already joined 10 city datasets to one building in 2.4 s and found its first real conflict: "50 Church St" with 78 units in the tax roll, "400 Duboce Ave" with 79 units in permits. We build the way Checksum ships: every claim cited, every struck line becomes a test, and only measured numbers on stage.
Why it isn't genericIt isn't a chatbot. It checks each building against named regulations and shows pass, fail or unknown, and every claim is marked VERIFIED, UNVERIFIABLE or CONFLICT. The building explodes in 3D on the judges' table.

Vidit's bar

Not generic

Do

  • Regulation is the feature. Every building gets pass, fail or unknown against named codes, each with its citation.
  • The output is a pre-plan in the format the standard defines, not a chat answer.
  • Markers drawn in NFPA 170 fire-safety symbol style (our own drawings), like a real pre-plan sheet.
  • Every number on screen is measured or sourced and dated.
  • One cinematic motion, the explode. Everything else holds still.

Don't

  • No chat box, and no "AI assistant" framing. The AI stays invisible.
  • No purple gradients, frosted-glass cards or "Powered by AI" badges.
  • Never say "live" for feeds that lag.
  • No made-up floors, prices or percentages.
  • No generic map dashboard: one building, one decision.
