FireSight

Know what's inside before the wheels stop. FireSight takes a dispatched address and shows the officer the building in 3D, floor by floor, with what the public record says about it. Every fact shows its source and date, and anything unknown is labelled unknown.

Please read: this is an independent prototype built by a team of four on Oct 22, 2026 at a San Francisco hackathon. It is not affiliated with SFFD or any fire department, it is not for operational use, and the example call below is simulated. We want to hear from people who do the job: what's useful, what's missing, and what would be dangerous.

Much of what changes tactics already exists in public records: construction type, soft-story status, rooftop solar, unit counts, open fire code violations and past fires. But it's spread across many city websites that nobody can search on the way to a call. Hand-built pre-plans take time and go stale.

What it does

From dispatch to a briefed crew

Address in

Dispatch gives an address. FireSight matches every city record to the one building, even when the records use different addresses for it.

Building in 3D

The real footprint and measured height, split into floors. One tap separates the floors so the hazards show where they sit.

Facts pinned in place

Construction, units per floor, soft-story, rooftop solar, violations, past fires, the nearest hydrant and cistern. Each one is dated and sourced.

Gaps said out loud

Stairs, standpipes and anything with no public record are shown as unknown. FireSight doesn't guess.

Officer approves

The officer approves or strikes each line of the size-up summary before it goes to the crew. Nothing goes out without a human.

Honest limits

What FireSight doesn't know, and doesn't do

Data limits

  • Building heights come from 2010 laser scans; footprints are from Sept 2023.
  • Hydrant and cistern positions are from a 2018 city snapshot.
  • City fire incident data runs about 4 weeks behind, and calls about 8 hours. It is never live.
  • Unit counts per floor come only from unit numbers mentioned in permits, so treat them as a minimum.
  • There are no interior floor plans, so stairs, standpipes and layouts are unknown.
  • Public records can be wrong or out of date. Your eyes and your department's pre-plan come first.

What it doesn't do

  • It doesn't replace your size-up, your 360 or your department's pre-plans. It's a head start on the way there.
  • It doesn't predict fire behavior or make tactical decisions.
  • It doesn't show personal information about occupants or building managers.
  • It doesn't send anything to a crew without an officer approving it.
  • It only covers San Francisco today, using the city's public data.

Code checks, with citations

FireSight checks some rules against the public record and shows pass, fail or unknown with the citation. Examples: the California annual fire inspection for apartments and hotels (Health and Safety Code 13146.2); rooftop solar access pathways (CA Fire Code 1205), which show as unknown when there's no roof layout; and how many of the standard pre-incident plan elements (NFPA 1620, now NFPA 1660) public data can fill. For 400 Duboce that's 7 of 21 filled, 9 partly.
