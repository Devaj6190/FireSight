# FireSight — live talk

Target: 60–90 seconds, accompanying Devaj's video. 169 spoken words; roughly 68–84 seconds at 120–150 words per minute, plus pauses. These are timing estimates; two timed spoken rehearsals remain for Rohan.

## Spoken script

A published NIOSH investigation into a fatal fire in Spring Valley found: “The fire department had not conducted a formal pre-incident inspection of this facility nor developed a formal pre-incident plan.”

An officer has minutes in the engine. Useful building records sit across city systems.

FireSight joins them into a cited, floor-by-floor 3D pre-plan. Paste a dispatch and see the records attached to that floor.

At 140 New Montgomery, that reveals a dated battery permit on floor 22. Tap for its record, date and placement rule.

Trust comes from showing our work. Every claim is labelled verified, unverifiable or conflict. Missing stairs stay unknown. Conflicting heights stay visible. The officer checks each brief line; saved strikes become regression tests.

Our intended buyers are pre-plan software vendors, such as First Due, Tablet Command and ESO. We'd supply the resolved building layer through an API or MCP.

Today, four San Francisco buildings demonstrate the workflow. It's a prototype, with source dates and limits on screen.

Size-up starts before the wheels stop.

## Delivery notes

- Pause after the NIOSH finding and before the closing line.
- Use the video for the detailed rules, sources and eval beats; do not narrate its entire sequence again.
- “Verified” means a cited public record supports the claim. It does not establish current on-site conditions.
- If asked about freshness: “Refreshed from data.sf.gov every 30 minutes; each source is as fresh as the city publishes it.”
- The vendor names identify prospective buyers, not customers or partners.
- Do not quote an eval score or model performance number until Lane C publishes the actual run.
- This prototype is not for operational use. No claim that it would have prevented the Spring Valley death.
